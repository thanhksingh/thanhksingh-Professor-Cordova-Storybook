Chapter One · Words Left Unsaid

To the most exceptional leader Virginia Tech has to offer, and the greatest professor the College of Business that UCF could ever offer. This may not be what you expect, so please take some alone time before reading, and I only ask that you please read until the very end. I apologize for being unable to deliver the farewell gifts, and 'Thank You' note sooner. I wish I could have seen you earlier, but the incident left me too ill and weak, and the shorter class periods during the summer had already added enough strain on you. Taking a step back from all the grief and regret, you probably didn't expect this, but it was inevitable. You might even blame yourself for not knowing, or for failing to act sooner. Maybe you'll take the time to read this, or perhaps not. I have always lived with an uncertainty, wondering if my absence was truly felt, or if my distance was simply easier to accept.

Chapter Two · The Mask

But know this, I was already walking down a dangerous road, an all too familiar path, and the only one I've ever known, depression. I don't like being me. I am always at war with social anxiety, fear of rejection, and a lack of belonging. Growing up, I never had friends, often isolated, heavily depending on comedic relief to actively gauge reactions just to understand social cues to fit in, wearing a mask to hide everything on the inside. I cannot fit in this society, one that requires you to be seamlessly social. My family refuses to admit this, but I am autistic, and there are too many signs that point to it. Most of my actions and how my life turned out can easily be explained by this. You probably don't remember the tell-tale signs from the office visits, but I do. Sometimes, I feel less than human, that I don't belong on this planet. There has always been a small part of me that wants to see myself fail, a deep-seated, unshakable hatred. I have become exceptionally adept at concealing my pain, ensuring the agonizing reality of how I truly feel remains entirely unseen.

Chapter Three · The Quiet Decline

My anxiety and depression would eventually reach the apex, ne plus ultra by April 19th. I would rarely consume food or water until I just stopped entirely between March and April, a time when the nights seemed to only grow colder. This caused a lot of internal damage to the point where my body was quietly giving up. It was impossible to tell what I was going through. I was too scared to tell you how dangerously close I came to the very end on April 21st. You were so happy, and I just couldn't take that away from you, even if it could be my last moment. There is still a part of me that hesitates to share this at all, a part of me that simply wanted to spare you from the reality of my silence.

Chapter Four · An Unexpected Home

Yet, even within that silence, that is exactly where you came in. Unbeknownst to you, the time you spent with me genuinely helped me through a difficult period, a time when it was easy for me to lose focus in a pit of anger, regret, and sadness. What started as a simple search for guidance for the Case Competition became something else entirely. You provided a safe haven no other could, a mother's dwelling in the midst of the raging storm. I remembered you said that you didn't want to seem too 'motherly' in jest, but, it was the greatest appreciation I had of you. Out of all the Integrated Business courses, your class was the only one I genuinely and full-heartedly looked forward to, excited for it to begin every Wednesday. The vast majority of my visits after that were just carefully planned excuses to return. The truth was, there was no other place I belonged, and nowhere else I wanted to be. Your office was the one place I didn't feel like an outcast.

Chapter Five · The Fool

Knowing how much that space meant to me, I really wish I could've treated you better. I always had a tendency to push others away to hide an internal struggle. I passed by your office once in February, and I saw that look, a great sadness in your eyes, and so I chose to stop by anyways. But, I did not do enough when I should have. I wish I could have done more in that moment rather than just stopping by, but building that kind of connection is incredibly difficult for me. I am constantly at odds with myself, terrified to get close to anyone. I struggle deeply to regulate my emotions, terrified that if I truly let my guard down, I will become completely overwhelmed. All because if I got too close, for once I would feel alive. I despise my own hesitation, and I deeply regret not finding the strength in that moment. I'm also sorry I couldn't live up to the wonderboy you thought I could be.

Chapter Six · The Unseen Burden

My inability to be that person started months prior as 'it' came back stronger than ever. My depression and social anxiety had a stranglehold on me before the Fall semester of 2025, which is why I couldn't live up to your expectations for Scanning the Horizon. That semester marked the darkest point of this entire struggle. Consumed by a profound sense of worthlessness, I was convinced I could never succeed by myself, an isolating belief that only pulled me further into the abyss. The mental toll continued to compound until I finally understood the absolute impossibility of carrying such a heavy burden alone. But, you didn't know, and I should've told you what I really meant when I sent that email during December of 2025. I truly struggle to ask for help, as I often feel like my troubles will fall into deaf ears. I am profoundly out of touch with my own emotions, making it incredibly difficult to open up due to a deep-seated fear of how I will be perceived. I was scared to feel alive, like one day I can lose it all easily so there is no point. Hopeless. Pathetic.

Chapter Seven · Out of the Cold

While that profound hopelessness no longer consumes me, I'm still going through the darkness, drifting side to side. And I don't know where I'll end up. However, the absolute worst of this is firmly in the past. The physical collapse from four months ago is over, and my body is out of danger. The internal storm has not fully quieted, but I am recovering slowly but surely. It is immensely difficult to take off the mask, but perhaps one day it will finally fall off, as I find my own way out of the cold. I know it is selfish of me to say this, but knowing you would depart from UCF hurt a lot. And, I know it is something I would've had to come to terms with eventually.

Chapter Eight · Until Then

I wish you could have seen me graduate along with getting a picture together. I deeply cherished the time and valued every moment with you, even if it was brief. I will miss you, deeply. You offered me the kind of unconditional care and protection that I thought only existed at home. Even if I hate being hugged, promise me that if we ever do cross paths, you'll hold onto me and never let go. Thank you for everything.

Written for someone who left the light on.
